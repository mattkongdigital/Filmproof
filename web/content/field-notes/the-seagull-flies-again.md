I built up a stockpile of 35mm film ahead of a holiday in Scotland. One of the
films was Lucky SHD 400. Lucky is a Chinese film brand with a reputation for
being affordable, if a bit unreliable in quality. That said, there was only one
way to find out.

I took a roll out for a test shoot with my trusty Pentax P30 when disaster
struck. The P30 would not wind forward. The mechanism had completely locked up.

It was heartbreaking. It was a week before I was to go wandering about Scotland.
Why me? What did I ever do? Why can't I have nice things?

I dropped the P30 off with a local repair shop and broke out the family-friendly
Olympus Trip as a substitute.

I am being dramatic. The Trip has done a good job and I look forward to sharing
the photos here. It was just such an adjustment to go back to a point and shoot.
I had to scramble about to get up close, or go up on my tiptoes to get the angle
I wanted. Or worse, squat. Since I'd made the step up to the P30, I'd made the
most of the lens and its zoom.

So, how did I end up buying a Seagull 4A? Another point and shoot?

I suppose you could say the stars had aligned. At least, that's what I said. To
myself.

I was trawling through eBay when I got the terrible news that the P30 could not
be repaired. Then the Seagull reared its head. It must have been a sign. Though
heartbroken, life must go on. Perhaps my heart could be fixed with a TLR medium
format camera.

Like any purchase on eBay, you can do as much due diligence as you want, but it
doesn't mean a thing until you have it in your hand. For film cameras, it's when
you get that first roll developed.
